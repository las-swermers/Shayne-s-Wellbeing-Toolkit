# Build the next tools from your existing resources

Start with one resource you own: PDF, slides, document, HTML, notes or screenshots.
Remove student names and real answers first. Include the source and any reuse
permission for third-party material. We can link to a resource or create an
original activity without copying protected worksheets.

## A small intake brief for each resource

1. What should a student understand or be able to do afterwards?
2. Intended ages, approximate duration, and individual or group use.
3. The source content and wording that must be retained.
4. The interaction and useful takeaway: reflection, plan, comparison or summary.
5. Whether anything needs saving, who can see it, and for how long.
6. Sources, reuse permissions and accessibility requirements.

## Suggested order

| Stage | First useful experience | Initial storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feelings explorer | Explore possible sensations, thoughts and supportive responses; optional knowledge check | No personal answers saved |
| Values card sort | Sort, reconsider priorities and choose one small action | Device-only or download |
| Roommate Agreement | Private preferences, a shared conversation, then a version everyone approves | Requires verified accounts and room membership |
| Study and stress tools | Short scenarios and a practical strategy plan | Save only deliberately selected information |
| Guided pauses | Choose a duration, follow audio/text, stop or skip freely | No private reflection tracking |

Do not build everything at once. Ship one polished short activity using the
same illustrated landscape, line icons, restrained motion and clean forms.
Reuse task-first layouts and previewable downloads. Keep additional explanation
optional; do not hide essential instructions or force mobile content onto one screen.

## Content and interaction principles

Feelings and values are not right-or-wrong answers. Body sensations can have
many explanations: do not infer or diagnose a feeling from a sensation. Score
only objective knowledge questions, not students' private experiences. Avoid
personality rankings, compulsory disclosure or penalties for missed days.

Prefer native short activities for a consistent, accessible experience. Before
embedding a third-party quiz, review data collection, cookies, age suitability,
accessibility and what is transmitted. A link-out may be more appropriate.
Do not execute arbitrary uploaded HTML inside the authenticated app: review it,
remove trackers and adopt a small allowlisted content format with versioning.

Card sorting needs keyboard/buttons as well as dragging. Audio needs a
transcript and stop controls. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Roommate Agreement boundary

A typed room number is not proof of membership. Use verified invitations and
explicit confirmation. Keep private drafts private to their author; reveal
only the agreed comparison/summary and let every participant approve its version.
Email must be an explicit action with verified recipients, not automatic sharing
of answers. Handle moving rooms, revoked access and deletion before launch.
